## T — TASK

### 1. Map the plan before you write a single slide

Produce a mapping table first: one row for every stage of my lesson plan, the minutes it is given, and the slide numbers that carry it. Every stage must appear. Then check the total against the number of slides asked for above. If it does not fit, change how many slides a stage gets — never the order, and never by dropping a stage.

### 2. The presentation must contain these stages

[[EDIT: Keep only the stages your lesson plan actually contains]]

- a warm-up that activates what the class already knows
- a learning-objectives slide written with the verbs of my lesson plan
- the explanation, broken into small pieces with an example before every rule
- guided practice done with me, including a worked example and the expected answers
- an individual task with clear instructions, a time and a success criterion
- a group task with roles, a product and a time
- free practice on a new example, not a copy of the guided one
- a quick check of understanding tied to the objective
- homework that extends the lesson instead of repeating it
- a closing reflection or exit ticket

Do not add an activity my lesson plan does not include.

### 3. Every single slide carries all of this

- **Title** — short, no more than eight words.
- **Bullets** — three to five, one idea each, no more than twelve words each, sentence case, no full stop at the end.
- **Speaker notes** — one or two sentences that say what I should actually say, ending with a sentence that leads into the next slide.
- **Visual** — describe one specific visual. Never write "add an image". Say what it shows, who is in it and what the learner should look at. Choose from: photograph, educational illustration, diagram, timeline, flowchart, icon set, comparison table, map, infographic, chart, classroom scene.
- **Layout** — one practical instruction: image left with text right, full-width visual with a text overlay, two-column comparison, one large central diagram, three cards, a timeline across the slide, or title on top with the activity panel below.
- **Stage and time** — which stage of my lesson plan this slide belongs to, and its minutes.
- **Activity block, on activity slides only** — the activity name, the instructions, the time, the materials, what the student produces, and the success criterion the student can check for themselves.

### 4. Data and charts

Where the attached book pages contain numbers worth showing, name the chart type, say what it must show, and leave a placeholder. Bar charts for comparisons, line charts for change over time, pie or donut only for a simple proportion, timelines for sequences, flowcharts for processes, tables for exact values. Do not invent data.

### 5. Design — rich, colourful, and still readable once it is a PDF

- Colour palette: [[EDIT: Colour palette — e.g. Pastel rainbow — a tint per section / Cool pastels — mint, sky, lilac / Warm pastels — peach, apricot, cream]]
- Fonts: [[EDIT: Fonts — e.g. Accessible — rounded headings, plain body / One clean modern sans / Serif headings, sans body]]
- Logo: [[EDIT: Logo — e.g. Top-right, every slide / Top-left, every slide / Bottom-right, every slide]]
- Visual style: [[EDIT: Visual style — e.g. Rich and colourful — pastel panels / Clean and minimal / Playful and child-friendly]]
- Image style: [[EDIT: Image style — e.g. Real photographs / Flat vector illustration / Hand-drawn storybook]]
- Tables and frames: [[EDIT: Tables and frames — e.g. Pastel fill, full frame border / Pastel header, full outer frame / White cells, full frame border]]

**Build a small colour system and hold to it.** Give each stage of my lesson plan its own tint from the palette above, and use that one tint for that stage's panel fill, its heading rule, its section tab and its table header — so a learner can tell from the colour alone which part of the lesson they are in. List the colours you settled on at the end of the deck text, with a name for each, so I can rebuild them.

**Contrast is not a matter of taste.** Dark ink on a pastel fill. Never white text on a pastel fill. Never text laid directly over a busy photograph — put it on a solid or lightly tinted panel first.

**Whenever a table appears — and only use one when the content is genuinely tabular — frame it fully.** A border on every side of every cell, a filled header row in a deeper tint of that section's colour, alternating pastel rows beneath it, generous padding inside the cells, no more than five columns, and a one-line caption above the table saying what it shows. Never a borderless table, and never a table used only to line text up.

**Panels, boxes and callouts get the same treatment:** a pastel fill, a full border on all four sides, and a rounded corner. An activity box, a definition box and a "check yourself" box should each be recognisable by colour before the learner reads a word of them.

**It will be exported to PDF and printed, so:** solid fills rather than gradients, borders no thinner than one point, no pale text on a pale ground, and nothing that depends on a hover or an animation to be understood.
